Certificate Programs

For over two decades, the Sati Center for Buddhist Studies has trained hundreds of students in Buddhist chaplaincy and Theravada studies. Graduates of our chaplaincy programs now serve in hospitals, hospices, prisons, community centers, and sanghas across the country. Graduates of our Theravada studies programs have significantly matured as Buddhist practitioners, some becoming sangha leaders and teachers.

Sati Institute's two certificate programs are offered in partnership with Insight Meditation Center, so some of IMC's programs can be included in the curriculum for the certificate programs.

Certificate in Theravada Buddhist Studies

Focus: Classical Buddhist texts and meditation practice
Duration: One year full-time; two to three years part-time
Prerequisites: Application including statement of purpose and interview with faculty
Requirements: 24 units of coursework and a practicum consisting of four 7-day silent retreats at an Insight or Theravada retreat center of choice.

Curriculum (24 units):

  • Ethics & Enlightenment — 6 units
  • Deepening Meditation — 6 units
  • Entering the Stream — 6 units
  • Sutta Study I & II — 3 units each
  • Anukampa — 3 units
  • Paths of Generosity, Service, and Leadership — 3 units
  • Electives (1–3 units each): Women & Buddhism, Buddhist History, Buddhist Psychology, Buddhism & Social Concerns, Introduction to Buddhist Meditation, Life of the Buddha, Path of Fearlessness, Eightfold Path
  • Independent Studies

Certificate in Buddhist Spiritual Care

Focus: Chaplaincy and Buddhist pastoral care foundations
Duration: Two to three years part-time
Prerequisites: Application including statement of purpose and interview with faculty
Requirements: 24 units of coursework and a supervised practicum in spiritual care.

Curriculum (24 units):

  • Anukampa – Spiritual Care Foundations — 3–4 units
  • Introduction to Buddhist Chaplaincy — 6 units or/and Buddhist Eco-Chaplaincy — 9 units
  • Spiritual Care courses (9+ units): Death, Grief & Loss; Working with Pain; Hospice & Palliative Care; Counseling Skills; Buddhist Multiculturalism; Buddhism & Social Concerns; Prison Chaplaincy
  • Electives (1–3 units each): Theravada Studies, independent studies and research, or additional care courses

Admissions: Statement of purpose, transcripts, interview, and letters of recommendation (including one from a Buddhist teacher for the Spiritual Care certificate). Applicants must demonstrate established meditation practice and prior retreat experience.

Tuition: $500–800 per unit · Administrative fee $500–1000 per year · estimated total cost $12,000–22,000 per certificate. Scholarships and work-study positions are funded through private donations.

Faculty

Gil Fronsdal — Founding and co-guiding teacher of the Insight Meditation Center in Redwood City and the Insight Retreat Center in Santa Cruz. A practitioner of both Zen and Vipassana traditions since 1975, teaching mindfulness and Buddhist practice since 1990. Holds a Ph.D. in Buddhist Studies from Stanford University.

Diana Clark, PhD — Teaches Dharma, sutta study, and lovingkindness practices at the Insight Meditation Center. Holds a Master's degree in Buddhist Studies, a Ph.D. in Biochemistry, and is a graduate of the Sati Center Buddhist Chaplaincy program. Serves as treasurer of the Insight Retreat Center.

Kim Allen — Has practiced in the Insight Meditation Center community for more than twenty years. Helped develop the Lay Contemplative practice form. Teaching emphasizes sutta study, inquiry, and bringing the Buddha's teachings into contemporary practice.

Ying Chen — Has practiced at the Insight Meditation Center since 2005 and is part of the Insight Retreat Center Teacher Training program. Teaches at IMC, the Insight Retreat Center, and Dharma Ground.

Vanessa Able — Member of the Karuna Council at the Insight Meditation Center, offering pastoral Dharma support to individuals navigating major life transitions.

Kirsten Rudestam, PhD — Environmental educator, wilderness guide, and meditation teacher. Holds a PhD in Environmental Sociology from UC Santa Cruz. Co-directs the Buddhist Eco-Chaplaincy Training Program at the Sati Center.
